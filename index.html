01 / THE QUESTION

The problem isn't a lack of greenery. It's connectivity.

Singapore already has green spaces, Nature Ways and ecological corridors. Yet hard urban infrastructure can create gaps in the resources available as pollinators move between habitats.

Eco-Spine does not aim to add greenery indiscriminately. It aims to strategically supplement existing habitats where infrastructure may interrupt ecological connectivity.

05 / BIOLOGY

Designed around the biology

🐝

Bees

The prototype focuses particularly on solitary bees, which use a range of nesting environments. Interchangeable cavities are proposed to provide nesting opportunities for selected cavity-nesting bee groups. Planting modules could provide floral resources.

🦋

Butterflies

Butterflies require different resources at different life stages. EcoFrame therefore combines nectar sources for adults with suitable host plants for caterpillars.

EGG→CATERPILLAR→PUPA→ADULT

06 / TARGETED BIODIVERSITY

Why aren't birds part of the target?

Eco-Spine takes a targeted approach rather than attempting to attract all wildlife. Birds may feed on insects the project aims to support, damage vegetation, and create contamination, maintenance or operational concerns around MRT infrastructure.

This is a site-specific design decision, not a judgement of birds. Bird observations remain valuable ecological records.

09 / SINGAPORE CONTEXT

Why Singapore?

In a land-scarce city, existing infrastructure may create opportunities to consider ecological functions without requiring additional ground-level land. Singapore's wider planning includes green-space connections, Nature Ways, Nature Corridors and a City in Nature vision.

Case study: Tengah

Tengah includes a 5 km Forest Corridor and is served by the developing Jurong Region Line. It is a potential future context for considering ecological integration alongside transport infrastructure — not an Eco-Spine site or endorsement.
